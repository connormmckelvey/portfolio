At Employer: UT Austin, Being Creative UGS Class with Professor Dempster 
Job Title: Principal Investigator and Technical Designer
Challenge/Problem with Context: 
-	As AI generates increasingly creative ideas, society undervalue AI led innovation due to bias. Can I develop a robotic system that enables us to standardize physical medium so that we can meaningfully test whether this anti-AI creativity bias exists. 
Action (what steps you put in place):

•	Designed fabricated a 2 degree of freedom robotic drawing arm using 3D printed parts and embedded electronics to standardize the study’s physical stimuli
•	Developed end to end software pipeline to simplify images, convert them to normalized coordinates, and use inverse kinematics to generate custom g-code robot instructions. 
•	Developed simulation software to visualize the robot path of drawing images simplified using Ramer-Douglas-Peucker algorithm before running on physical hardware
•	Executed a two block psychometric study to measure the perceived creativity of human vs AI design artwork across 25+ participants

Low-cost Vibrotactile Feedback Band for Large Motor Skill Learning
At Employer: Human Enabled Robotics Lab at Texas Robotics under Professor Ann Fey and Dr. Ross Neuman
Job Title: Undergraduate Researcher
Challenge/Problem with Context: 
-	Current solutions to teach large motor skills like a golf swing relies on solely visual feedback, this restricts both the quality and speed at which individuals can learn and retain motion skills. 
-	Populations with vision issues are restricted from the current methods for teaching these skills
-	Current tools the focus on the sense of touch are restricted to one skill or type of motion 
Action (what steps you put in place):
•	Invented a low-cost, modular band system that can be placed on any limb to deliver direction based haptic cues to guide motion through up to 4 haptic motor pods, including a Master Pod which holds a custom designed PCB featuring integrated charging, acceleration and orientation tracking, and wireless communication with your laptop
•	Implemented a limb tracking, computer vision software that sends commands wirelessly to the band to guide the user in a specific path. 
Result(s) – quantify with $, % or #:
•	Built a full vibrotactile feedback system for under $25 per unit, making it a low-cost solution for learning motor skills.
•	Designed a 3cm x 5cm PCB capable of controlling 4 motors, collecting acceleration & orientation data, and implementing one of the fastest wireless communication protocols for microcontrollers with latency around 1ms.
Strategic Impact on Employer:
 Enabled anyone (including those with vision issues) to learn large motor skills like kicking a soccer ball, or proper gym form using directional, haptic feedback. 
Themes (your strengths, skills, personality traits): 
 Fast Prototyping, PCB Design, CAD Design, Low-latency Communication Protocol
